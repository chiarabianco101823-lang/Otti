# Otti
Visual Screening AI Assistant

1. Project Overview

Visual Screening AI Assistant is an educational AI-powered assistant designed to help users identify possible signs of refractive errors and visual disturbances through a structured conversational screening.

The project was developed by students studying Optics as part of the bioERGOtech educational experience.

The assistant collects information about the user’s visual symptoms, habits and vision-related difficulties. Based on the answers, it provides an educational assessment and explains whether a professional eye examination may be advisable.

The system is intended for educational and informational purposes only. It does not replace an optometrist or ophthalmologist and cannot provide a medical diagnosis.

2. Problem Statement

Many people experience visual symptoms such as blurred vision, headaches, eye strain or difficulty focusing without knowing whether these problems may require a professional examination.

The project addresses this issue by developing an accessible conversational tool that helps users describe their visual difficulties and understand possible reasons for seeking a vision assessment.

The assistant also supports students in applying their knowledge of optics, optometry and common refractive errors to practical situations.

3. Project Objectives

The main objectives are:

* To create an interactive AI assistant for preliminary visual screening.
* To collect information about common visual symptoms.
* To identify possible indicators of myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism and presbyopia.
* To explore possible binocular vision problems and other visual disturbances.
* To explain the relationship between reported symptoms and possible visual conditions.
* To encourage appropriate professional eye examinations.

4. How It Works

The assistant interacts with users through a conversational chat interface.

The screening process follows these steps:

1. The assistant welcomes the user and explains the purpose and limitations of the screening.
2. It asks questions about age range, visual habits and current use of glasses or contact lenses.
3. It investigates symptoms such as blurred distance vision, blurred near vision, headaches, eye strain, double vision, glare and difficulty seeing at night.
4. It asks whether symptoms affect one or both eyes, when they occur and how long they have been present.
5. It considers contextual factors such as prolonged screen use and the date of the last eye examination.
6. It organizes the collected information and generates a final educational report.

The final report includes the main reported symptoms, possible visual issues to investigate and a recommendation regarding the urgency of professional assessment.

5. Technologies and Tools

The project uses:

* n8n: workflow automation and AI agent orchestration.
* Chat Trigger: receives messages from users through the chat interface.
* AI Agent: manages the screening conversation and generates the final report.
* OpenAI Chat Model: processes user responses and generates explanations.
* Hosted Chat: provides the interface through which users interact with the assistant.

6. Safety, Limitations and Ethical Considerations

The assistant is not a diagnostic medical device.

Its responses are based exclusively on information provided by users and cannot establish the presence of a refractive error or ocular disease.

The system must not:

* Provide definitive medical diagnoses.
* Prescribe glasses, contact lenses or treatments.
* Recommend medication.
* Reassure users that their vision is normal based only on their answers.

Persistent or unexplained visual symptoms should be assessed by an eye-care professional.

Sudden vision loss, severe eye pain or sudden double vision require urgent medical assessment.

The project also considers privacy and data protection. Users should avoid sharing personal identifying information or unnecessary sensitive medical details.

AI-generated responses may contain inaccuracies. The results must therefore be interpreted critically and must never replace a professional examination.

7. Testing and Evaluation

The prototype was designed to support a structured conversation and provide understandable explanations based on the information collected.

The main evaluation criteria include:

* Correct understanding of user responses.
* Appropriate identification of reported symptoms.
* Clear explanations of possible visual conditions.
* Consistency of the final report.
* Respect for medical safety limitations.
* Appropriate recommendations for professional assessment.

8. Project Impact

Visual Screening AI Assistant demonstrates how artificial intelligence can support educational activities in the field of optics and optometry.

It encourages users to pay attention to their visual well-being while helping students develop digital skills, critical thinking and a better understanding of the relationship between visual symptoms and possible refractive or functional problems.
